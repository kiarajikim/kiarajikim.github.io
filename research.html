Buyer-Initiated Auctions: Nominations in Oil & Gas

Job Market Paper

PDF

Abstract

A seller offering many items may not know which ones buyers want to buy. Pre-auction nomination is a common solution in oil and gas leasing that hands that burden to the buyers instead: a tract is offered only if some bidder pays a nonrefundable fee and commits to buy it at a reserve price (the minimum bid). I develop a two-stage nomination-auction model in which bidders' values are correlated but costly to learn. On arriving, each bidder chooses whether to learn early and nominate, or to wait and learn only about what others have nominated. Using novel data from North Dakota lease sales in 2017–2018, I estimate the model and compare nomination with a design in which the seller chooses what to offer. Nomination raises seller revenue by about $3.05 million (44%) across the 728 tracts in the sample. Most of the gain comes from the nominator's commitment to buy, by ensuring every offered tract sells and adding competition that raises the winning bid.

AI and the Labor Market: A Worker's-Eye View

with Nathan Mester and Greg Sun · GWCS Best Paper Award

SSRN Media Coverage

Abstract

We collect and analyze data from a survey of just under 2,000 U.S. citizens to study perceptions about the role of generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) in the workplace, as well as changes in career-relevant decision-making since the initial launch of ChatGPT. We report three main findings. First, most respondents report dedicating more time to learning now compared to before ChatGPT, and this trend is driven by those who use AI and believe that it is a useful tool for learning. Second, while time invested in learning tends to decrease with age, this trend is attenuated among those who believe in AI's potential as a learning tool. Third, workers who believe that AI is a complement to their own effort increase their effort in response to the introduction of AI while workers who believe that AI is a substitute show no such effect. These patterns are robust to the inclusion of a rich set of controls and demonstrate the importance of worker-level tradeoffs in shaping heterogeneous responses to the introduction of AI. They highlight career-long learning as a particularly important dimension of AI capabilities that is especially important in the eyes of many workers.

Auction Bundling Under Interdependent Cost

Abstract

This paper uses a dynamic structural model to characterize and quantify cost interdependency in mineral rights using sequential auction data to exploit dynamic spatial incentives arising from sequentially realized winning outcomes. I find that bidders overbid in anticipation of future rent from winning close-by auctions. Structural estimates indicate that winning multiple nearby tracts results in cost synergy, with variation in proximity and size. Using the results, I show that bundling auctions improves both efficiency and revenue in a sequential auction setting. A back-of-the-envelope calculation suggests that revenue and efficiency improve if tracts 0.25 miles apart are bundled. Furthermore, the size of a bundle should be capped at 400 acres in total.
