Buyer-Initiated Auctions: Nominations in Oil & Gas

Job Market Paper

PDF

Abstract

Markets served by auctions can fail when too few bidders participate. Pre-auction nomination is a common but understudied remedy in oil and gas lease sales: bidders pay a nonrefundable fee and commit to purchase at a minimum price to propose tracts for sale, and only nominated tracts are auctioned. I study nomination when bidders' values are positively correlated and learned only at a cost. Correlation makes a nomination informative: it signals to uninformed bidders that their own values are likely high, drawing them in and thickening the market. But the same signal can make waiting optimal, so fewer bidders learn early and fewer tracts are nominated. To sign the net effect, I characterize a two-stage nomination-auction game, derive equilibrium learning and nomination strategies, and structurally estimate learning costs and value dependence using North Dakota's state oil and gas auctions. Counterfactuals show that nomination works mainly as a screening device. Relative to a no-nomination benchmark, it raises revenue by about $3,090 (32%) per sold auction, and by 46% overall once its higher sale rate is included. Roughly 80% of this comes from screening on demand, which guarantees a committed bidder, and the rest from screening on value. The market-thickening signal matters only for the lowest-value tracts.

AI and the Labor Market: A Worker's-Eye View

with Nathan Mester and Greg Sun · GWCS Best Paper Award

SSRN Media Coverage

Abstract

We collect and analyze data from a survey of just under 2,000 U.S. citizens to study perceptions about the role of generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) in the workplace, as well as changes in career-relevant decision-making since the initial launch of ChatGPT. We report three main findings. First, most respondents report dedicating more time to learning now compared to before ChatGPT, and this trend is driven by those who use AI and believe that it is a useful tool for learning. Second, while time invested in learning tends to decrease with age, this trend is attenuated among those who believe in AI's potential as a learning tool. Third, workers who believe that AI is a complement to their own effort increase their effort in response to the introduction of AI while workers who believe that AI is a substitute show no such effect. These patterns are robust to the inclusion of a rich set of controls and demonstrate the importance of worker-level tradeoffs in shaping heterogeneous responses to the introduction of AI. They highlight career-long learning as a particularly important dimension of AI capabilities that is especially important in the eyes of many workers.

Identification of Interdependent Cost Using Dynamic Incentives with Application to Auction Bundling

Abstract

This paper uses a dynamic structural model to characterize and quantify cost interdependency in mineral rights using sequential auction data to exploit dynamic spatial incentives arising from sequentially realized winning outcomes. I find that bidders overbid in anticipation of future rent from winning close-by auctions. Structural estimates indicate that winning multiple nearby tracts results in cost synergy, with variation in proximity and size. Using the results, I show that bundling auctions improves both efficiency and revenue in a sequential auction setting. A back-of-the-envelope calculation suggests that revenue and efficiency improve if tracts 0.25 miles apart are bundled. Furthermore, the size of a bundle should be capped at 400 acres in total.

Market Responds the Morning After: How Abortion Regulation Changes and Age-Limit-Reduction of Emergency Contraceptive Pills Affected Market of the Morning-After Pills

with Cecilia S. Diaz-Campo · 2nd-Year Paper 2024 Charles Leven Memorial Prize

SSRN

Abstract

The 2012-2016 time period is when there were changes to both the supply and demand side of the over-the-counter (OTC) Emergency Contraceptive Pills (ECP) market. The supply side shock has to do with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration granting ECP the right to be sold in any retail store shelves, first to the branded version of ECP and eventually to the generic variants. This meant more stores can now sell ECP and thus better accessibility of ECP from the consumers' perspective. The demand side shock is the 2013 Texas House Bill 2 Act that caused number of abortion facilities operating in Texas to decrease by half. Using simple Regression Discontinuity analysis and Triple Difference in Differences analysis on price of Emergency Contraceptive, I find suggestive evidence of 1. dynamic responses within retail chains to demand and supply shocks in terms of pricing and selection of products and 2. worse accessibility of ECPs for low income families.
